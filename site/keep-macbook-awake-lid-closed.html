How to keep a MacBook awake with the lid closed — without an external monitor

Close the lid on a MacBook and it goes to sleep within seconds. Downloads stop, SSH sessions drop, and any AI agent you had running dies mid-task. Here's why that happens and every way around it, from Apple's built-in clamshell mode to a free app that handles it in one click.

Why a MacBook sleeps when you close the lid

macOS treats a closed lid as a direct request to sleep. It isn't an idle timeout you can extend — it's a separate path called clamshell sleep, and it fires immediately regardless of what's running. That's why tools that merely "prevent idle sleep" do nothing once the lid is down.

Option 1: Apple's clamshell mode (needs power + a display)

Apple does support running with the lid shut, but only when both of these are true: the Mac is connected to power, and an external display, keyboard or mouse is attached. Unplug either and it sleeps. For a laptop in a bag, on a desk with no monitor, or on battery, this doesn't help.

Option 2: caffeinate (doesn't survive the lid)

The built-in caffeinate command and most menu-bar apps (Amphetamine, KeepingYouAwake, Caffeine) create a power assertion. That stops idle sleep — the screen stays on while you're away — but a power assertion cannot override clamshell sleep. Close the lid and the Mac sleeps anyway.

Option 3: pmset disablesleep (works, but dangerous)

The only thing that survives a closed lid is a system-level flag:

sudo pmset -a disablesleep 1

This genuinely keeps the Mac awake with the lid shut and nothing attached. Two problems. First, you have to remember to run sudo pmset -a disablesleep 0 afterwards or your Mac will never sleep again. Second — and this one is nasty — it also switches off macOS's own low-battery emergency sleep. On battery, the Mac will run until it hits 0% and hard-shuts-down. We found this the hard way: one overnight run, a dead battery, and battery health dropped from "Good" to "Poor".

Option 4: NoSleep (does the pmset dance for you, safely)

NoSleep is a free, open-source menu-bar app that wraps pmset disablesleep in the guardrails it's missing:

  • One toggle (⌃⌘S) turns closed-lid keep-awake on and off, and always restores normal sleep when it's off or the app quits.
  • Battery guard releases the block at 5% so the Mac can sleep normally instead of dying.
  • Smart mode watches for running AI agents (Claude Code, Codex, Ollama, Cursor…) and lets the Mac sleep as soon as they go idle, so you don't have to remember to turn it off.
  • Timers for 15 min / 1 h / 2 h if you just need a download to finish.

It's native Swift, universal (Apple Silicon and Intel), and asks for your password exactly once.

Which should you use?

  • Docked at a desk with a monitor and power? Apple's clamshell mode is fine.
  • Need the screen to stay on while you watch something? caffeinate or any keep-awake app.
  • Need the Mac to keep working with the lid actually closed — in a bag, on battery, no monitor? You need pmset disablesleep, and you want something that turns it off for you and protects the battery. That's what NoSleep is for.
